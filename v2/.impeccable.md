## Design Context

### Users
Pakistani e-commerce merchants (fashion, electronics, home, beauty) who ship via COD using multiple couriers. They are small-to-mid-size business operators — often the founder or ops lead — who currently juggle 5-6 courier portals, Excel sheets, and WhatsApp to run fulfilment. They work in high-pressure, high-volume environments (especially during sale days) where a delayed payout or lost parcel directly costs them money and customer trust. They use SmartLane on desktop during work hours.

### Brand Personality
**Reliable. Direct. Technical.**

SmartLane is infrastructure — it should feel like Stripe for Pakistani logistics. Not flashy, not warm-and-fuzzy, but undeniably competent. The interface should communicate: "we've thought about this so you don't have to." Copy is honest and specific (mentions real courier names, real pain points, admits trade-offs on pricing).

### Emotional Goals
Merchants should feel:
- **Control & confidence**: "I know exactly where my parcels and my money are at all times"
- **Relief & calm**: "I can stop chasing couriers and start selling"
- **Speed & efficiency**: "This is faster than anything I've used before"

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean, utilitarian, data-forward. White backgrounds, blue accent (#00559F), monospace eyebrows for structure. Dense but not cluttered.
- **Typography**: Instrument Sans (headings) + Inter (body) + JetBrains Mono (data/labels). Fluid sizing with clamp().
- **Theme**: Light mode only. White (#ffffff) background with subtle gray (#f8fafc) sections.
- **References**: Stripe's documentation clarity, Linear's information density, Vercel's typographic precision.
- **Anti-references**: NO cheap Shopify-template energy, NO stock photos, NO 3D illustrations or animated backgrounds, NO gradient-everything, NO blue-suit corporate jargon, NO generic SaaS templates.

### Design Principles
1. **Show the data, not decoration** — Every visual element should convey real information. The dashboard illustration in the hero works because it shows actual data patterns. Avoid decorative flourishes that don't serve comprehension.
2. **Be specific, not generic** — Name real couriers (TCS, Leopards, Trax). Show real order IDs. Quote real pain points. Specificity builds trust faster than polish.
3. **Density over sprawl** — Pakistani merchants are used to dense information environments. Don't over-space or over-simplify. Respect their ability to scan data quickly.
4. **Honest over aspirational** — Acknowledge trade-offs ("we'll be direct about pricing vs. unsustainable cheap rates"). Avoid superlatives and vague promises. The product speaks for itself.
5. **Infrastructure aesthetic** — SmartLane is a layer between merchants and the courier network. Design should feel like reliable plumbing, not a consumer app. Monospace tags, status pills, and table-like layouts reinforce this.
